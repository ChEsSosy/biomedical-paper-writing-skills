# Review synthesis and sentence-preserving editing

Use this guide when planning a Review argument or choosing prose revisions. It supplies writing decisions within the existing Phase 7A/7B contracts; it adds no schema fields, runtime operations, or author authority. Apply only the parts relevant to the user's requested edit.

## Choose the writing task

| Request | Writing action | Execution boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Make existing sentences more professional | Improve expression within each sentence, preserving its propositions, position and citation attachment | Use only permitted Light operations; leave structural suggestions outside the candidate |
| Improve paragraph logic or synthesis | Diagnose the question, evidence comparison and paragraph relationship; propose the smallest useful change | Medium stays within its approved paragraph; Deep requires exact structural decisions and closure |
| Broaden discussion or use more literature | Identify relevant unused evidence in the admitted set and explain its contribution | Scope, synthesis and citation decisions return upstream; missing evidence becomes a targeted retrieval need |
| Translate a Chinese Review into English | Preserve proposition correspondence and distinguish translation from structural editing | Translation intent alone does not authorize new claims or relax runtime admission |

A Review profile is optional. Without one, use clear biomedical academic expression and the supplied argument/evidence. Do not invent profile IDs or describe editorial judgment as a measured corpus feature. These guidelines do not make an ordinary editorial suggestion a validated producer package.

## Turn evidence into an argument

For a theme or argument unit, identify the question the reader needs answered and the strongest statement supported by the admitted evidence. Determine which observations agree, which differ, and whether the compared systems, interventions, comparators and endpoints permit the proposed comparison. Select details that establish the finding, explain a material difference, clarify measurement, or preserve a decisive counterexample.

Use these as decision prompts, not a mandatory paragraph sequence. A definition paragraph, mechanistic explanation and study comparison have different jobs. An evidence summary can remain descriptive when a broader conclusion is unsupported.

Named studies can be compared within a question-led synthesis. For example, contrast what two assays measure and why their findings cannot be directly pooled, rather than giving each paper an unrelated miniature abstract. Retain the study-specific conditions and attribution needed to understand the comparison. This is theme-based organization; standalone paper-by-paper organization retains the frozen contract's chronological, historical or method/source-inventory scope. Author-name counts do not determine whether a paragraph is synthesis.

Keep source claims, cross-source synthesis, author positions and future directions distinguishable. An apparent explanation for disagreement remains a hypothesis unless the supplied evidence resolves it. Publication counts do not establish independence or consensus. A useful author framework organizes comparisons across sections; proposing to rename or replace it remains an author decision.

## Give each paragraph a clear job

Identify the paragraph's central judgment or explanatory task, the background needed to follow it, and the relationship that makes the next paragraph relevant. Use the existing outline's fields and proposal rationale where applicable; do not add ad hoc schema fields.

When a paragraph mixes several substantial tasks, propose a supported split or relocation with a reason. When adjacent paragraphs repeat the same task, identify exactly what could be combined and which distinctions must remain. Preserve source-to-claim attachment through any proposed reorganization.

Transitions should name the relationship: a new endpoint, a different model, an unresolved explanation, a counterexample, or the next design decision. Use causal or consequential transitions only when that relation is supported. Do not append a generic caution or transition to every paragraph.

## Present limitations where they affect interpretation

Keep the full evidence conditions in the evidence records. Decide what the reader needs locally:

| Kind of boundary | Presentation decision |
| --- | --- |
| A detection limit, confounder, missing comparator or model difference changes the nearby inference | Keep it beside the affected result or comparison |
| A shared scope condition applies to several findings | State it where its scope is clear; retain local reminders where omission would invite a different interpretation |
| A generic caution repeats an already clear boundary without adding a proposition | Flag it for possible consolidation under the requested content-editing scope |
| A negative result, disagreement or unresolved mechanism | Preserve it and its scientific scope; fluency does not justify dropping it |

A phrase such as “does not establish” may carry essential scientific content. Judge its function before editing. Do not impose deletion ratios, quotas for caveats, or a fixed “finding–interpretation–limitation” rhythm.

Consolidating wording must preserve every distinct condition and the reader's ability to associate it with the right claim. In Phase 7B, the actual footprint and protected-change rules still govern: an edit requiring prohibited citation/anchor changes or an altered approved claim must return to the author for an updated source/upstream artifact. A Deep label or general preference for fewer caveats cannot authorize those changes. Under a same-sentence request, report cross-sentence consolidation as a suggestion only.

## Check coverage, framework and future directions

Compare each heading's promise with the endpoints and questions actually covered. Offer concrete choices when coverage is uneven: narrow the heading, clarify the section's role, or propose evidence-backed additions through upstream review. A heading retitle is an editorial recommendation for the author to resolve in the upstream source and outline; the current Phase 7B operation vocabulary does not apply heading-text changes. Bind any later prose revision to the newly identified source and approved outline. Distinguish research measurements used to interpret a study from product quality-control requirements. Do not transfer a threshold or regulatory claim between them without supplied support.

Show whether a central framework represents an observed relation, cross-source synthesis, or an author proposal. Use it only where it helps explain a comparison or decision. Preserve the author's chosen definitions until a terminology change is authorized.

Describe the actual source-selection basis and its known scope. A narrative source set does not imply a systematic search. Project-specific evidence labels can describe source identity or attribution capacity; study-quality rankings require an adopted assessment method. Increasing citation count alone is not an objective: identify what an additional admitted source would contribute.

A future direction should connect a recorded gap, conflict, limitation or author objective to a needed observation/comparison and explain what judgment its resolution could change. Select relevant directions rather than routinely adding AI, clinical or regulatory sections. Candidate comparison tables may be planned in the outline; current Review-to-M05 execution accepts figures, not a general table-production interface.

## Preserve meaning within the same sentence

For a same-sentence edit, retain all source propositions and their logical relationships. Check the subject, action, object, conditions, comparator, direction, degree, time, statistical meaning, negation scope, certainty and causal strength. Preserve numbers, units, source identity and citation attachment. A protected terminology change additionally needs the exact external mapping and permission required by the contract.

Improve familiar professional collocations, modifier placement, pronoun reference and avoidable wordiness. Keep an already effective sentence. More abstract nouns, stronger verbs and longer syntax are not quality goals. Use surrounding text to resolve an explicit referent; ask a focused question when more than one interpretation remains plausible.

| Proposed edit | Required judgment |
| --- | --- |
| “the trade-offs that can be accepted” → “acceptable trade-offs” | Usually a local compression; preserve any stated actor or condition |
| “recipient cells” → “target cells” | May narrow the cell population; do not assume equivalence |
| “governs” → “influences” | May weaken the asserted relation; professionalization alone gives no reason to change it |
| “does not establish Y” → “supports A” | Replaces a negative boundary with a positive proposition; not a same-meaning substitution |

A better paragraph structure is a separate suggestion when the user requested only wording. Translation also needs proposition-level review and cannot be counted as a controlled test of English-to-English style improvement.

## Review the candidate at the relevant level

For an expression edit, compare the original and candidate for meaning, clarity and natural usage. For synthesis, examine the question answered, study comparison, transitions and coverage while checking retained conditions and evidence relations. Record an unresolved interpretation as an author query; unchanged text is a valid outcome.

Use a small before/after example and rationale when review is requested. Deliver clean prose when that is the requested product; keep required formal manifests in their existing artifacts. Deterministic checks establish only their implemented scope, not semantic equivalence, synthesis quality or scientific truth.
