# Section and Paragraph Roles

Use this reference for reverse outlines, mini-outlines, and proposed paragraph plans.

## Section responsibilities

- `abstract`: report the bounded problem, approach, principal supported findings, and supported conclusion; do not introduce unsupported novelty or mechanism.
- `introduction`: establish context, located prior evidence, a precisely bounded gap, and the study question. The gap must be answered by later evidence.
- `methods`: describe what was done with enough identity to interpret evidence; do not carry result claims.
- `results`: report observations, comparisons, statistics, and limited result-level takeaways. Reserve broader interpretation for Discussion.
- `discussion`: interpret supported findings, compare alternatives, state limitations and validation needs, and remain inside claim ceilings.
- `conclusion`: synthesize only author-approved claims at their approved scope.

These are responsibilities, not mandatory heading names or a fixed paragraph count.

## Reverse outline

For each existing source paragraph record:

- stable paragraph ID and locator;
- current role;
- claims and evidence currently used;
- whether the paragraph mixes incompatible roles;
- whether it maps to the current section goal.

Do not rewrite the paragraph during this step. For structured notes with no manuscript order, record the explicit notes-only skip rather than inventing a current outline.

## Proposed paragraph plan

Each paragraph serves one primary role and contains:

- paragraph goal;
- source paragraph IDs;
- claim IDs and evidence IDs;
- Figure IDs, if any;
- transition goal;
- whether material reordering occurs;
- author approval state.

Splitting or merging paragraphs is a structural proposal. Preserve source IDs so the move is reversible. Material reordering stays pending until the author approves it.

## Common structural findings

- claim appears before its evidence is introduced;
- Results paragraph contains a mechanistic or clinical extrapolation;
- Discussion repeats observations without interpretation;
- Introduction gap is not answered by any experiment;
- paragraph combines unrelated claims or comparison directions;
- limitation or negative evidence is detached from the claim it bounds;
- Figure order and paragraph order imply different evidence hierarchies.

Report these as located findings, not automatic edits.
