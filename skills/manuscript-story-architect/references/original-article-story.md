# Original Article Story Reconstruction

Use this reference when proposing the overall Article architecture. The output is a plan, not manuscript prose.

## Stable anchors

Lock five elements before proposing structure:

1. central question;
2. take-home message;
3. author-supplied or author-approved novelty scope;
4. key claim IDs;
5. key boundary IDs.

Every textual anchor needs provenance, source locators, and approval state. The architect may restate an existing proposition for clarity, but may not create a new scientific proposition or novelty claim.

## Build candidates without changing science

A structural candidate may change only grouping, order, emphasis, compression, section allocation, paragraph role, and Figure sequence. It may not change claim scope, evidence strength, causal or mechanistic level, treatment of negative evidence, or novelty.

Create multiple candidates only when they expose a real author choice, such as observation-first versus mechanism-first ordering supported by the same approved evidence. State the tradeoff and the affected IDs. Do not choose for the author.

## Story chain

For each key claim, verify this chain is visible:

```text
question → observation → evidence → bounded interpretation → limitation → validation need
```

If a link is missing, retain the gap rather than bridging it with prose. A validation need names an evidence category or unresolved boundary; it is not a concrete experiment by default.

## Journal adaptation

Journal instructions may alter structure, emphasis, compression, and order only. Record the profile and instruction locators. Keep `scientific_authority: false`. If a journal convention conflicts with the scientific boundary, preserve the boundary and flag the conflict.

## Approval package

Present the exact plan version, material moves, alternative candidates, unassigned evidence, open blockers, and decisions requiring approval. Approval never transfers automatically between candidates or artifact types.
