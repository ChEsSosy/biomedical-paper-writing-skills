# Observation–Interpretation–Boundary–Validation

Use one card per key claim or tightly coupled finding group.

## Observation

Record what the supplied result shows under the measured comparison. Include source locators and avoid mechanism, importance, or generality not present in the measurement.

## Interpretation

State the most conservative meaning that remains within the supplied evidence. Record causality, mechanistic, and hedging levels separately. If the author has not supplied an interpretation, an architect proposal remains pending and must be traceable to the observation it restates.

## Boundary

Give the boundary a stable ID. Record:

- claim ceiling;
- population or model limit;
- assay or method limits;
- prohibited extrapolations;
- unresolved uncertainties;
- approval state.

Apply ceilings by dimension. Record causality and mechanistic specificity separately from biological scope, generalizability, in-vivo relevance, and clinical relevance. Do not summarize mixed-dimensional evidence with a single global `low`, `moderate`, or `high` label.

The boundary is part of the scientific content. It cannot be removed merely to improve narrative flow.

## Alternative explanations

Include only author-provided alternatives, alternatives directly implied by located method/model limitations, located literature alternatives, or explicitly marked architect proposals requiring author review. Do not populate a generic list to make the card look complete.

## Validation needs versus experiments

`validation_needs` describes an abstract evidence deficit, such as independent replication, orthogonal support, causal validation, in-vivo relevance, or scope extension.

`proposed_validation_experiments` is empty by default. A concrete experiment may appear only when it is author supplied or was explicitly requested in a separate experiment-design task. Preserve its source locator and origin. The story-planning task itself does not authorize experimental design.
