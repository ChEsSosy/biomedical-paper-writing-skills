# Animal Experiment Profile

Use for in-vivo animal interventions, disease models, pharmacology, physiology, behaviour, imaging, and tissue endpoints.

Check:

- species, strain, sex, age or developmental stage, model induction, treatment, timing, and endpoint when reported;
- experimental unit: animal, litter, cage, tumour, repeated measurement, or another declared unit;
- group allocation, randomization, blinding, inclusion/exclusion, attrition, and sample-size definition when supplied;
- vehicle, sham, baseline, genotype, positive, rescue, and matched controls as applicable;
- repeated-measures or hierarchical structure and whether statistics match the unit;
- welfare-related or reporting limitations only when relevant to interpretation and present in the source.

Claim ceilings:

- intervention effects can support causal claims within the tested animal design when assignment and controls justify them, but not an unqualified molecular mechanism;
- one model, strain, sex, or disease induction does not establish broad biological generality;
- an animal phenotype is not clinical efficacy or human safety;
- tissue or surrogate endpoints do not automatically establish organism-level benefit;
- multiple measurements from one animal do not create independent biological replicates.

Keep model validity, translational limits, attrition, and unit-of-analysis uncertainty explicit when supported by the supplied material.
