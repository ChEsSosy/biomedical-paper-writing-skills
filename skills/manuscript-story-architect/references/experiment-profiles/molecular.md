# Molecular and Structural/Biophysical Evidence Profile

Use for biochemical assays, molecular perturbations, binding measurements, interaction assays, structural methods, and biophysical characterization.

Check:

- molecule, construct, isoform, modification, reagent, and assay identity;
- purified-component versus cellular context;
- direct and indirect readouts;
- negative, positive, competition, specificity, loading, rescue, and orthogonal controls as applicable;
- concentration, stoichiometry, kinetics, affinity, activity, and normalization definitions when reported;
- whether the assay measures binding, abundance, localization, activity, modification, or downstream response.

Claim ceilings:

- co-occurrence, co-localization, co-immunoprecipitation, or proximity does not automatically establish direct binding;
- binding does not automatically establish functional activity or mechanism;
- abundance or modification change does not automatically establish pathway activation;
- a structure or predicted pose does not establish cellular effect or in-vivo relevance;
- one assay modality does not establish specificity when plausible alternatives remain untested.

Keep assay-specific limitations and construct/context differences attached to the claim they bound.
